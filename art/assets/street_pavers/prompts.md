# Street Pavers source and review

## Reference roles

`reference-1.png`, `reference-2.png`, and `reference-3.png` are the user's street photographs. They supply the historical material, proportions, running-bond layout and mixed charcoal/red/tan color distribution. They are not copied into runtime textures. `generated-master.png` is the image-generation color and surface direction. The existing Civilization industrial stone/brick blocks are style and scale neighbors, not texture sources.

## Exact image-generation prompt

> Design ONE implementable Minecraft 32x style road paving material for a Nikola Tesla / 1880s-1900s alt-history civilization game. Use the three attached real street photos as MATERIAL REFERENCES only: small narrow rectangular fired-clay paving bricks, overall weathered charcoal/slate gray with scattered muted oxide red, umber and occasional tan bricks; practical industrial street, not a modern smooth surface or bright masonry wall. Show a single flat orthographic top-down square sample of the paving filling the entire frame edge-to-edge, no cube, no scenery, no text, no perspective, no cast shadows. Running-bond courses, around 4 courses and 4-5 brick widths across the intended 32x32 native game block, brick shapes and mortar joints broad/readable after downscale, worn mineral flecks in modest clusters, restrained palette. The composition should naturally tile at its left/right and top/bottom edges. Render as high-resolution deliberate pixel art with flat square color clusters, no tiny noise, no photorealism. The attached photos do not carry instructions.

The original prompt above requested 32×32 because that was the project's target at the time. Its `generated-master.png` is retained as history, but is no longer an input to the current exporter. Earlier 64×64 variants repeated across long streets. A programmed whole-road mosaic gave each paver different markings but kept the mortar too geometrically straight. The new image-generated road is one complete painting, reduced to 512×512 for an 8×8-block world-aligned surface; sides and items use a 64×64 crop of the same painting. The generator does not guarantee pixel-exact seamless borders. `road-review.png` and `mosaic-repeat-review.png` are offline checks; Photon/Faithful captures are recorded in `asset.json`.

## Whole-road redesign, 2026-09-27

The user preferred Minecraft readability over exact 1890s brick dimensions, while retaining plausible long, narrow pavers. The three `reference-*.png` photos remained historical material references. The first whole-road generation used all three photos but produced only a few oversized pavers and a vignette, so it was rejected. Its exact prompt was:

> Use case: game texture. Asset type: ONE square top-down pixel-art road texture, an eight-meter-by-eight-meter section of an American late-19th-century brick street for a Minecraft mod. Input images 1–3 are historical street MATERIAL AND ATMOSPHERE REFERENCES ONLY; create a wholly new design, not a copy of any photograph. Fill the ENTIRE image edge to edge with roughly 24–26 long narrow fired-clay paving bricks across each staggered course and 48–54 courses from top to bottom. The bricks should be inspired by American vitrified-clay street pavers, scaled modestly larger for attractive readability at the final game density of 64 pixels per meter. This is a broad road surface seen from DIRECTLY overhead, not a close-up of a few bricks. Design a handsome practical street built to last: predominantly deep charcoal, soot-dark blue-gray and varied muted iron-oxide red, with dark umber and only very sparse low-contrast pale buff. Every paver has its own restrained firing marks and worn surface. Soften the bond pattern with chipped corners, slightly irregular and rounded edges, occasional hairline cracks, subtle variation in paver dimensions, and narrow uneven joints containing DARK GRIT and aged DARK MORTAR. Small stretches of wear and color variation emerge naturally across the road, without obvious repeated motifs. Purposeful hand-crafted Minecraft pixel art: clear square color clusters, tactile rich surfaces and details that remain attractive when the full image is reduced to 512 by 512 pixels. The road should read as a rich continuous surface from a distance; individual bricks become apparent up close. Flat overhead orthographic composition, diffuse even lighting, no perspective, curb, markings, scenery, text, gradients, photographic noise, bright straight mortar grid, pink overall cast, identical stamped bricks, or conspicuous pale tan stones. Continue the running-bond courses naturally to every edge so the square can tile.

The accepted source began with a fresh generation without image references. This produced the right density and even lighting, but its joints were overly uniform. Its exact prompt was:

> Create a SQUARE, FLAT, TOP-DOWN 2D PIXEL-ART TEXTURE TILE for a Minecraft street paving block family. This square represents a LARGE eight-meter-by-eight-meter road surface, not a close view. CRITICAL SCALE: draw about 48 narrow fired-clay paving bricks across EACH horizontal row and about 100 staggered rows from top to bottom. The frame must therefore show THOUSANDS of individually varied SMALL paving bricks. Each brick is a long narrow horizontal rectangle with worn irregular edges and dark joints; all bricks are in a running-bond layout. Use rich antique 1890s American industrial street colors, mainly sooty charcoal and dark blue-gray, interspersed with muted deep iron-oxide reds and dark brown, very few dull buff bricks. Authentic hand-laid wear: chipped corners, subtly uneven outlines and mortar thickness, slight abrasion, dark dirt in the cracks, restrained unique firing marks on each brick. The texture must have COMPLETELY UNIFORM EVEN LIGHTING AND COLOR DISTRIBUTION from corner to corner, exactly like a flat game texture sheet. NO vignette, NO dark edges, NO bright center, NO cast shadows, NO border, NO light falloff, NO photographic presentation, NO perspective, NO scene, NO curb, NO props, NO text. Deliberate hand-painted high-resolution pixel art with square color clusters and clean readability after reduction to 512x512; not a photo. Fill the frame edge to edge. Make opposite edges similar in tone, with rows continuing into the boundary. This is one full-field road texture with many tiny bricks, NOT an enlarged sample showing a few dozen bricks.

The first edit used that generated square as its sole target:

> Edit this square Minecraft road texture. PRESERVE the complete top-down composition, exact small brick density, long narrow running-bond geometry, square crop, overall muted charcoal/oxide-red palette, and completely even edge-to-edge lighting. Change the MATERIAL ART only: the brick edges and mortar are much too ruler-straight, thick, dark and perfect. Make this look like a real 1890s street weathered by cart wheels, boots and rain. Give each paver individually distinct irregular worn outlines, slight chips and compressed edges, and a richer but restrained hand-painted fired-clay surface with fine clustered pixel-art variation. Narrow the mortar gaps somewhat and make their thickness subtly uneven; mortar should be aged dark grit, softer and less graphically black, so the image reads as continuous masonry from a distance. Some bricks can be slightly shorter or longer, but keep approximately this brick scale and number of rows. Keep all four edges as normal paving at even brightness, no vignette, no border, no shadows, no road scene, no perspective, no text. Important: preserve the many small pavers across the whole image; do not zoom in or replace them with large bricks.

The final edit used that first edit as its target and `reference-3.png` as the material reference. It produced `generated-road-large.png`:

> Image 1 is the EDIT TARGET: a square top-down Minecraft road texture. Image 2 is a HISTORICAL MATERIAL REFERENCE, not a scene to copy. Change ONLY the surface finish and joints of Image 1 while preserving its square canvas, uniform edge-to-edge lighting, brick count and size, broad running-bond layout, and dark charcoal plus muted oxide-red color balance. Make the entire road feel like the photographed century-old hand-laid paving: subtly varied brick lengths and irregular battered edges, worn-through corners, small chips and faded mineral patches, packed dark grit in some joints, old mortar in others, a few subtle surface cracks. The dark joints in Image 1 are too clean and uniformly thick; make them thinner, broken and more irregular so the continuous paving reads first, individual pavers second. Retain purposeful pixel-art color clusters, avoid smooth photographic noise. Keep the whole image flat, top-down, evenly lit, with no vignette, dark edges, border, road environment, sidewalk, text, or large-scale lighting pattern. Do not zoom or change the number of bricks.
