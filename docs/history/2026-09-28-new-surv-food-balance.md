At most 100 cooked meat portions were produced but not eaten during the observed period, with 66,600 kcal of nominal potential. This is **production minus recorded consumption**, not a verified current inventory: trading, discarding or storage contents were not audited. Roughly 5,500 kcal per active hour were spent, so that potential is on the order of 12 active hours at the observed pace. Meat alone greatly outpaced immediate need before the first field harvest. Berries were another easy early source and merit separate comparison.

The current livestock loot tables produce roughly two meat per cow/pig/sheep kill. A one-meat baseline for these animals, leaving cooked-food kcal unchanged, would have yielded 71 portions and 47,300 kcal from the same kills (46% less meat energy). Changing only cooked beef/pork from 700 to 500 kcal would have yielded 65,500 kcal (26% less energy), while still producing 131 portions. These are counterfactuals using this playthrough's kill counts, not tested balance results.

**Recommendation for the next trial:** reduce *wild* livestock to one meat per kill before lowering cooked-meat value. This preserves the value of cooking and meat as compact food, while curbing surplus generated by incidental frontier hunting. A later feed-at-breeding/production husbandry rule could offer a higher domestic yield in exchange for crop inputs; that distinction is still a proposal. Reducing meat alone cannot create a reason to breed animals until the feed/yield loop exists. Recheck with a mature wheat field and another survival session before altering calories and drops together.
