# Heat and thermal comfort



## Temporary disable switch

`config/civilization-heat.toml` contains `enabled = true` by default. It is a per-instance/common configuration used by the authoritative server; restart after changing it. Civilization Dev is enabled again after the airship performance trial so cold exposure can be tested there. While disabled, source discovery/emission and diffusion stop; new fuel waste heat is discarded instead of building a catch-up queue. Existing saved heat and pending budgets remain intact. Comfort bonuses, cold drain, rain pauses on heated work faces and thermal machine efficiency adjustments are neutral, the survey is empty, and `/civilization heat` reports that heat is disabled. Restoring `true` and restarting resumes the saved field and rebuilds source discovery from loaded chunks.

## Initial thermal field

Overworld climate comes from biome, altitude, the 60-minute day and regional rain. Rain approaches up to a 6Ã‚Â°C cooler district baseline over five real minutes and recovers over forty minutes after it stops; the biome profile scales or excludes this direct rain response. The regional clock owns this background; no block-level temperature history is created merely by weather or daylight changing. The local field stores thermal energy relative to the current background, so an unheated cell immediately reads the current regional climate. At ordinary elevation, a river biome is about 63Ã‚Â°F at noon and 45Ã‚Â°F at midnight. This is a game-tuned boundary condition, not a simulation of ground thermal inertia.

Rain cooling is 80% of the district value in dry/savanna profiles, 60% in humid profiles, 50% along coasts and oceans, zero in cave profiles, and full strength elsewhere. Above Y=80, air cools 0.045Ã‚Â°C per block of elevation; this does not replace the biome's mountain profile. Unrecognized modded biomes use their own index and the temperate swing. Local fires, buildings and heat transfer then alter those background values.

Each active block cell has energy and a material-dependent heat capacity; its displayed temperature is background plus energy divided by capacity. Sources, air, walls and player-built chimney pieces all occupy the same one-block lattice. Partial blocks use their actual eight cut-piece materials for capacity, and the occupied pieces on each 2Ãƒâ€”2 face determine how much air can pass through it. Closed full blocks conduct without airflow; open doors, open trapdoors and empty space let air mix. No room, duct or named chimney is detected. Placing or breaking a block changes that cell's capacity and interfaces, so it changes the resulting temperature and flow.

A frozen field snapshot exchanges energy between neighboring cells and publishes the complete result. Indoor air mixes 15% of a difference per quarter step, or 19% upward if lower air is hotter, with up to 4% additional buoyant rise. In enclosed air this extra flux is limited by the warmer-lower temperature difference, preventing artificial ceiling concentration. Unobstructed sky columns instead carry warm excess upward with matched incoming/outgoing face flux; an explicit atmosphere outflow closes the world-height boundary. Air in a sky-exposed column mixes 10% sideways and carries 16% of its warm excess upward. Both paths transfer heat between adjacent cells without creating it, and their combined per-step outward rates remain below one. This favors upward exhaust through an open shaft without stripping all warmth from an enclosed room. Solid-to-solid conduction is slower (3% of the lower conductance), while a hot solid face transfers to adjacent air at 10% of the square root of conductance. Exposed air exchanges about 1.8% of its excess with the regional atmosphere per simulated second. A tiny exposed-air difference below 0.05Ã‚Â°C and numerical residuals are retired to an atmosphere ledger. These rates are deliberately coarse rather than real fluid dynamics. A cut-block shaft works by material conduction, face openings and ordinary air transfer; it is not granted a hidden outlet bonus.

A coal machine emits environmental heat as its purchased reserve is spent. One Mineral Coal carries 13,000 game heat units for industrial sources, independent of the work recipe. Household and prototype cooking stoves emit 5% of that local waste heat (650 units per coal; about 32.5 units/second at full productive burn), doubling the previous stove emission to make working beside it noticeably warm. Their fuel work credit and processing times are unchanged. This stove factor is applied when the reserve is spent, including already-loaded coal; existing room warmth dissipates normally. A continuously productive industrial coal fire therefore releases about 650 units per loaded second; ordinary idle burn releases one tenth of that. The coal burn rate, machine work credit and processing times have not changed. The Smithy's diamond job runs four times as long as iron at the same productive burn rate, so its total coal use and environmental heat are four times higher, without multiplying heat a second time. The source queue limits release to 800 units per simulated second and retains any remainder for later. Industrial batch releases can therefore trail actual completion. Existing saved fire reserves retain their already-purchased remaining heat; new coal uses the new amount. Old saved fire reserves with no emission ledger do not create a second emission.

A registered machine allocates the *same finite release* to two physical entries: 80% at actual open top outlets and 20% evenly across all matching structural blocks, including the controller. Each outlet receives its share in its first adjacent air cell. Source placement no longer scatters exhaust up an eight-block column: the solver must carry heat through every subsequent cell. Every part of the shell can warm adjacent room air through ordinary face exchange. If all top outlets are blocked, their share remains in the machine body rather than being deposited into a solid roof; heat never jumps outside. A player-built enclosed shaft channels rising hot air through its physical openings, while a machine exhausting into an unvented room gradually overheats that space. No named chimney or room is recognized. Torches supply 6 units per loaded second, lit campfires and ordinary/soul fire blocks 24, and lava is a maintained hot reservoir limited to 48 units per exposed face per second. The Oil Engine releases heat from fuel it actually consumes. All player comfort still comes from the temperature of the air at torso height, with a separate felt-temperature response over 2.5 seconds; there is no proximity-only warmth bonus.

The local field, pending fuel heat and timestamped environmental releases are saved in format v3. Existing v2 heat and fuel queues are retained, with a new timeline beginning at the current loaded server time; only the older v1 absolute-temperature history resets. V3 retains queued inputs even when saving halfway through an exchange: partial computed results are discarded on restart and their still-queued inputs are replayed once. The clock rebases on restart without simulating offline or heat-disabled time. Blocks, inventories, machine fuel and player data are unchanged. Back up the heat file before an established-world upgrade; an older JAR cannot preserve the new timed-release history on downgrade. Unloaded chunks are neither forced to load nor simulated; their stored warmth and pending machine emissions wait for reload. Existing source discovery is chunk-indexed and bounded, and its emission queue spreads work fairly across ticks.

The current solver still performs a whole-field exchange in bounded batches under a 5 ms soft budget per server tick. It advances fixed quarter-second physical steps, stages only the timestamped releases due in that step, and publishes atomically. Releases arriving during a job wait in future frames; they no longer accumulate directly in an older field snapshot. A delayed worker resumes its next due step immediately rather than waiting for another global phase boundary. At equal simulated time, prescribed input history produces the same field regardless of worker slicing, including a mid-job save/reload. Actual severe overload can still delay the field; this is not a guarantee of real-time service or identical temperature-dependent gameplay decisions made from stale readings. Material edits, climate changes and source feedback still use the loaded world's current state rather than a historical world replay.

The local production candidate uses a 5 ms soft deadline, with an upper ceiling of 65,536 operations per call. Completing a step can start the next overdue step within that same deadline and operation ceiling. The former 16,384 ceiling could itself force ordinary 30k-cell fields below four steps per second even before the time budget was exhausted. Trials at 3 ms and 4 ms failed scheduled laboratory latency checks; retaining 5 ms is an explicit CPU tradeoff, not a city-scale performance claim. Capturing cell keys, array/map growth, individual world reads and JVM pauses can still exceed the soft deadline. Limits per dimension are 262,144 active cells, 262,144 timestamp/position input entries, and 4,096 pending machine-source locations. Finite machine batches wait for release-queue headroom before their pending budget is spent. Maintained natural inputs that cannot fit are accounted to the existing atmospheric overflow ledger. Weak settled cells may also be retired to that ledger to make room. The helmet's solver age measures the actual difference between world time and the committed physical step, rather than just time since the latest publication; `/civilization heat` also reports timed-release count. Section-local scheduling, per-section memory quotas, stale-data handling by individual district, and representative 200-player profiling remain open; this build does **not** claim that scale.

Each exchange uses dense numeric slots for energy and transfers, with section-local coordinate indices that promote from sparse maps to arrays as occupancy grows. The workspace is reused while active and released when idle. Native block-state material and face geometry are resolved once per exchange; mixed cut blocks and custom slab geometry remain position-specific. Loaded native chunk sections are read directly, with loaded status refreshed after each yield; debug/custom levels and custom chunks retain ordinary world reads. No chunk is force-loaded and no material/geometry cache survives into a later exchange. Transfer coefficients, cell retirement order, physical clock and v3 save format are unchanged. Section-local lookup reduces exchange cost; it does not yet schedule sections independently.

Ordinary and soul fire blocks, lit normal and soul campfires, ordinary and soul torches, and exposed natural or placed lava warm loaded neighboring cells through the same field. Extinguishing or removing a source ends new emission while stored warmth decays. Redstone torches have no combustion heat. Flame/lava discovery and emission have separate bounded queues; broad lava fields can take time to index. There is no wind, smoke, indoor room classifier, radiant player shortcut, offline city simulation or sky-city thermal guarantee.
## Dedicated thermal laboratory

`./thermal-lab.ps1 Seed` creates a separate, reusable Creative world in ignored `runs/thermal-lab/server/thermal-laboratory`. `./thermal-lab.ps1 Server` opens it on **localhost:25573**; type `stop` in that console to save and close it. It uses the matching development build and never deploys over Prism or touches an existing player world. A worktree has its own runtime, world and build output. The laboratory helpers are development-source-only and are absent from the distribution JAR.

The ten signed stations sit at Y=80: torch, campfire, ordinary fire, contained lava and redstone-torch control at X=0/24/48/72/96, Z=0; sealed wood, sealed brick, roof-vented brick, outdoor Foundry and roof-vented brick Foundry at the same X positions, Z=24. Rooms have real doors. All sources use normal discovery and scheduled diffusion. Foundries start ignited with eight real coal and 32 raw iron; their normal tickers buy reserves, process recipes and emit waste heat. The supply chest at 45,80,12 holds a thermal helmet, coal, raw iron and flint and steel. The day is fixed at noon, initial regional weather is clear, and fire spread is disabled to preserve the comparison buildings. Buildings are comparisons, not special thermal room types.

Operator commands in this dedicated development server:

- `/thermal_lab visit <station>` teleports to a signed station; `/civilization heat` reads local conditions.
- `/thermal_lab sample` appends the fixed probes to the world's `measurements.csv`.
- `/thermal_lab rain` and `/thermal_lab clear` change the shared regional weather. Clear does not erase the district's existing cooling history.
- `/thermal_lab off` removes natural heat sources and extinguishes both foundries for stored-heat cooling tests. This persists; relight/rebuild sources manually afterward. No command automatically resets edited buildings.

`./thermal-lab.ps1 Check` creates a **different** world in `runs/thermal-lab/check`, measures 80 seconds of heating and 40 seconds of cooling, and shuts down. It records torso, plume, wall and above-roof probes every five seconds, ambient/actual/rise in Celsius, solver age/cell count, queued heat, stored field energy, the atmosphere ledger and machine fuel/work/output. It checks real coal consumption/output, positive torch/campfire/lava air warming, finite probes, timely solver progress and net cooling after shutdown. `./thermal-lab.ps1 Reload` checks exact saved cell and pending-heat persistence in a second server process. Check refuses to overwrite an existing measurement run; preserve it and use a fresh isolated run for a new baseline. Manual and automated servers share port 25573 and must run sequentially.

`./thermal-lab.ps1 Visual` uses a separate saved-world copy and the existing hidden, unfocused 1920×1080 mouse guards; screenshots are in `runs/thermal-lab/client/screenshots`. It does not change Prism settings. No client or world is automatically published.

`./thermal-lab.ps1 Physics -RunName <fresh-name>` runs deterministic physical experiments in another isolated world and writes `physics.csv` plus `physics-result.txt` there. It measures positive/negative material pulses, conservation and stability, six solid-conduction directions, door/trapdoor airflow, exact source budgets and removal, lava boundary exchange, equal-temperature material comparisons, wall thickness, warm-up/cool-down curves for seven room layouts, and controlled closed/vented industrial Foundries. Failed checks are collected rather than hiding later results. Existing result files are preserved: choose a fresh run name. `Check` and `Reload` also accept a matching fresh `-RunName` so the original scheduled baseline stays intact.

For solver optimization, run `./thermal-lab.ps1 Bench -RunName bench-before` before editing, then `Bench -RunName <fresh-candidate-name>` afterward. Dense air, scattered pulses and partial-block scenes each run eight quarter steps, with two warm-up rounds and three measured rounds. `benchmark.csv` records elapsed time, main-thread CPU time and allocated bytes using JVM counters. Saved baseline NBT results must match candidate cell membership and energy/atmosphere values within 1e-8; a mismatch fails the run. Baseline and candidate must use the same laboratory fixtures and build environment. `python tools/analyze_thermal_benchmark.py <baseline.csv> <candidate.csv>` compares equal work, prints median reductions and writes `benchmark-summary.json` beside the candidate. CPU counter resolution and other host activity limit precision; this benchmark measures kernel cost, while `Check` measures scheduled progress. Existing benchmark files are preserved.

Ordinary and soul fire now use the same 24-unit loaded-second environmental budget as campfires. Existing loaded flames are discovered through the normal chunk index; removing/extinguishing their blocks stops emission. This follows the actual vanilla flame lifetime, including its maintained netherrack/soul-soil behavior; it does not invent another fuel inventory or claim finite chemical combustion. Torches and campfires likewise remain maintained game sources. Lava is a maintained 1,000°C external reservoir: its bounded face exchange warms colder neighbors and absorbs heat from hotter neighbors. The diffusion solver excludes a second lava-cell exchange path, so a visibly hot lava block cannot simultaneously act as ambient-temperature storage. Heat absorbed into the maintained reservoir leaves the local field through that explicit boundary, rather than being atmospheric loss or machine work.

Enclosed-air convection cannot generate an artificial ceiling hotspot from uniform warm air. Unobstructed sky columns carry warm excess upward through consecutive faces, with matching inflow/outflow and a world-height atmosphere boundary; they do not use a named-chimney classifier. Source placement tests require zero heat above the actual outlet until transport runs. An industrial fire can still overheat a vented room. Old absolute temperature bands fitted to the former eight-cell exhaust scatter are not physical reference data: the replacement regressions require at least a 25% occupied-air reduction from the enclosed chimney, at least a 15% increase when the short roof flue is capped, exact supplied-energy closure and the existing upper-shaft limit. Temperatures remain recorded for later calibration. A chimney is a physical exhaust path, not a comfort guarantee.

Thermal mass and insulation are measured separately: timber can warm through sooner than initially cold brick, while transferring less heat at equal surface temperature. Joined native double slabs have their full material's capacity, conductance and closed faces; the empty-half-cell representation previously made them behave thermally like open air. Single top/bottom slabs combine half material with half air. Existing stored energy is retained, so corrected double slabs may read cooler when their proper thermal mass is applied; block shapes and items are unchanged.

`python tools/analyze_thermal_lab.py <physics.csv> --plot` validates a completed experiment file, writes `physics-summary.json` and renders `thermal-curves.png` beside it. Plotting requires Matplotlib; analysis without `--plot` uses only the Python standard library. The curves compare equal supplied budgets, distinguish heating from source-off cooling, and label simulated seconds and actual temperature rises.

The physical criteria follow [DOE's heat-flow description](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow) and [NIST's distinction between heat-release rate, fuel and ventilation](https://www.nist.gov/el/fire-research-division-73300/firegov-fire-service/fire-dynamics). The model implements a coarse conservative lattice and maintained source boundaries, not CFD, SI calibration, radiation, oxygen/smoke, phase changes or full internal machine temperatures. Legacy heat capacities and work credits still use game units. Remaining acceptance includes longer equilibrium, dynamic door/chimney/material edits, rain, unloaded areas, whole fuel/work/loss accounting and representative load. Machine work credit and environmental heat remain separate quantities; the measurements do not claim that the entire fuel-to-work energy balance has been reconciled.

## People and machines



Comfort follows a Gaussian curve centered on 70Ã‚Â°F with an 8Ã‚Â°F spread: 100% at 70Ã‚Â°F, about 46% at 60Ã‚Â°F or 80Ã‚Â°F, and about 2% at 48Ã‚Â°F. Felt temperature below 64.4Ã‚Â°F also spends calories once per online second in Survival: a quadratic ramp reaching 0.20 kcal/s at 35.6Ã‚Â°F or colder, with 0.05 kcal/s at 50Ã‚Â°F. `coldExposureMaxKcalPerSecond` in the calorie server config sets the cap. Heat disabled, unloaded/unsupported locations, Creative/Spectator and offline time have no cold charge; the reserve cannot go below zero. This is energy demand, not direct temperature damage. At full comfort, active survival players gain up to 20% cheaper block labor and travel, 15% faster block breaking, and 5% movement speed. Supported jump/attack calorie actions use the same discount. Healing and hunger-effect costs stay unchanged; depletion retains its existing restrictions. Sprint remains three times walking per block at equal comfort. The server updates air and felt temperature once per second; comfort and cold costs use felt temperature.



Kiln-family furnaces and workshops buy a temperature-adjusted work budget with each new coal. From 32Ã‚Â°F through 78.8Ã‚Â°F the base work budget is unchanged; warmer surroundings add at most 10%. Below 32Ã‚Â°F cold reduces it by up to 30%, with overhead shelter halving the cold penalty. The Fired Heater instead pays a correspondingly adjusted batch heat cost, checking sufficient physical coal before committing. Processing times and output quantities stay unchanged. Pump/engine work efficiency and claim upkeep are unchanged. Existing purchased work credit is retained.

Direct rain on the block immediately in front of a heated controller prevents ignition and pauses productive work in Kilns, Foundries, Cooking Stoves, Fertilizer Furnaces, town workshops and the Fired Heater. The existing regional `isRainingAt` query includes sky exposure and biome precipitation, so a roof over the work face is enough; an exposed chimney outlet may remain outdoors. Already-lit coal fires continue at the lower idle rate and resume their saved work when sheltered or dry. Oil Pumps, Coal Drills, the distillation column, condenser and Hot-Bulb Engines are not rain-paused by this rule. Rain still affects their surrounding air through district climate. The controller screen names the rain obstruction; the common ignition button rejects a wet heated face.



This is a bounded initial loss approximation, not a full internal machine-temperature/ignition/cooling simulation. Neighboring ambient/field temperatures are sampled around the controller. A roof is the initial direct shelter cue; actual wall insulation also affects the surrounding heat field. Tuning lives in `ThermalRules`; material lookup delegates to the [shared physical material compatibility policy](physics.md). Its legacy heat capacity remains distinct from physical mass and J/K capacity.



## Survival temperature HUD



A small brass/glass thermometer sits between the health and calorie bars, with local Fahrenheit temperature above it. It shows the player's felt temperature; hover in inventory to compare it with the measured torso air. No helmet is required. It follows survival HUD visibility and hides with F1, Creative/Spectator, or missing/stale temperature data.



Cold glass is muted blue; comfortable glass sage green; hot glass amber-red, with continuous interpolation by comfort. A restrained four-second breathing highlight strengthens with actual comfort bonuses and disappears while depleted. There is no extra meter or permanent percentage text. A tiny brick-pattern foot/chevron appears only when the existing brick-road contact rule is active, including supported cut-block surfaces; it disappears off-road or airborne. Neither display changes any bonus calculations.

A temperature color wash is drawn over the finished world and HUD, including open screens and tooltips. It is blue in cold conditions, distinctly warm golden tan near 70Ã‚Â°F, then eases toward red as heat reduces comfort. Changes settle over a few seconds rather than flashing when entering warm air. It uses the same server-sent felt temperature as the thermometer; unsupported or stale samples fade out. This is a client-side translucent color effect after shader rendering, not a mutation of Photon's internal white-balance setting or a change to heat simulation. `config/civilization-temperature-wash.toml` has local `enabled` and `intensity` controls (default on, strength 1; 0Ã¢â‚¬â€œ2 range). The effect intentionally covers interface colors as well as the world.



Open the normal inventory and hover the matching thermometer above its panel for labeled air and felt temperatures, comfort, any current cold calorie drain, work/travel calorie saving, mining speed, comfort movement and the separate road movement bonus. Depletion explicitly reports paused comfort benefits. Road and comfort percentages are not added together. The Thermal Survey Helmet retains its separate diagnostic thermal camera.



## Thermal Survey Helmet



Use `/civilization heat` for local air and felt temperatures, comfort and thermal efficiency.



Equip **Thermal Survey Helmet** in the head armor slot. Available in the Civilization creative tab or `/give @s civilization:thermal_helmet`; no survival recipe yet. It temporarily reuses the native iron helmet model/armor texture.



While worn, visible nearby block faces receive a strong translucent color from their actual sampled block temperature. The fixed Fahrenheit palette runs from blue cold surfaces through tan comfort, amber/orange heat and red/pale extreme heat; its labeled legend is deliberately nonlinear to give useful visual room near human temperatures while retaining an absolute meaning. Full blocks and physical partial-block shapes are tinted on their exposed faces. The block under the crosshair also gets an outline and approximate Fahrenheit temperature and difference from local air. At most 14 well-spaced arrows estimate the strongest nearby heat-transfer vectors from sampled temperatures and the shared exchange-rate rule; the overlay does not yet include the solver's partial-face or buoyancy terms. The readout shows age since the last completed field exchange and highlights delays above three seconds, so stale data is visible. Solid foreground geometry hides surfaces and arrows behind it. All player-facing temperatures use Fahrenheit; internal simulation and save units are unchanged. Remove the helmet to remove the overlay. This is a development inspection instrument, not an approved survival progression item. The underlying 1Ãƒâ€”1Ãƒâ€”1 sampling lattice stays fixed to world coordinates while the bounded viewing window follows the player. Only cells within 10 blocks of the player's torso are sampled, once every two seconds per wearer; the compact packet stores offsets from one world position and temperatures to 0.1Ã‚Â°C. The once-per-second comfort update remains separate. Fluid surfaces and purely animated model geometry are not yet reconstructed as thermal meshes.
