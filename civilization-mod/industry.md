# Deposits, refining and industrial liquids

The working progression is **stone tools + Coal → Kiln → bricks → Foundry → ingots → steel + Machine Parts → Oil Derrick → Fired Heater → Distillation Column → Air-Cooled Condenser → refined fuel → Coal Drill**. Refining also produces lubricating oil and sulfur for fertilizer. Build distinct vessels and connect their real ports with basic pipes. New worlds generate physical deposits directly. An opt-in saved-chunk oil retrofit is enabled for New Surv; other old worlds do not gain physical oil automatically. Uranium, vehicle tanks and freight balancing remain later work.

## Thermal processing and retired vanilla systems

Coal is the display name of the existing `civilization:mineral_coal` item. Machines and claims keep their existing fuel values and stored balances. Torches, soul torches, campfires and fire charges use it too. Finite coal loot is unified; wither-skeleton coal drops are removed. Coal/charcoal/coal-block production and conversion recipes are disabled, including compression and decompression. The dense geological coal seam is a separate extraction block and remains unchanged.

The Kiln now handles glass, stone finishes, terracotta/glazed terracotta, heat-produced dyes, sponge drying, nether bricks and popped chorus fruit. The Foundry also processes silk-touched coal/gem/quartz ores and ancient debris into netherite scrap. Transferred recipes retain their previous output quantities and smelting times, with production XP as described below. Existing clay-to-brick batches, metal/steel processing and stove recipes keep their existing balance. Ordinary sponge drying does not fill a bucket with water.

Vanilla furnace, blast-furnace and smoker processing is disabled even on existing/generated blocks. Their recipes and drops are removed, as are furnace-minecart crafting and engine operation. Coal blocks yield no item when mined. Retired items are hidden from creative tabs/search, and villager offers involving them are removed on access. Campfire cooking remains disabled; smoke/bee utility remains. Crafting tables, decorative shaping, banners, naming/book application, disenchanting and armor trims retain their distinct roles.

All five controller recipes that formerly required a furnace now use one cobblestone in that position: Kiln (nine cobblestone), Foundry (eight brick blocks around cobblestone), Fertilizer Furnace (four brick blocks/four copper around cobblestone), Cooking Stove (three copper above six cobblestone), and Smithy (existing surrounding materials, cobblestone center).

Existing worlds/inventories are not swept or converted: registered legacy IDs remain loadable, but old vanilla coal/charcoal cannot power custom machines and cannot become industrial coal. Existing retired processors retain accessible contents; old furnace carts can roll but cannot propel themselves. Administrator commands can still create legacy items. This is a curated base/mod recipe boundary, not a guarantee against a later datapack explicitly reintroducing recipes. Broader renewable-metal, special-equipment and freight audits remain separate work.

## Shared controller interfaces

Every current machine screen uses the same soft tan enamel/dark metal cabinet, brass nameplate, inset native slots and controls. Land, trade and survey controllers share the skin and widgets while retaining their specialized layouts. Thicker borders and enlarged brass pins extend outward from the native layout; raised beveled brass nameplates stay clear of slots. Category tabs use dark inset metal; recipe search has a magnifying-glass icon and muted placeholder. Corner details keep their scale when the panel stretches; the generated master, export pipeline and receipt live under `art/textures/ui`. The physical boat helm remains an in-world control, not an inventory screen.

- **Coal:** exactly two fuel slots in the Kiln, Fertilizer Furnace, Cooking Stove, Foundry, Tannery, Textile Workshop, Smithy, Oil Derrick and Fired Heater. The coal slots sit on the left, with a centered fire window and embedded striker on its right. Native click/drag/shift-click and sided hoppers use both slots. The slot layout does not change recipe/output capacities; current per-job fuel rates are listed below. Claim reserves and trade storage remain full chests.
- **Recipe drawer:** collapse/open with the side button, search by name and scroll compact item-icon/name rows (mouse wheel or Page Up/Down). Smithy category tabs filter Tools, Weapons, Armor and Components; material chips filter Iron, Gold, Diamond and Netherite using the equipment catalog. Irrelevant filter bars are omitted on simpler machines. Netherite rows are labeled as upgrades; Repair and Automatic are separate icon controls with hover labels. Filters never hide recipes merely because materials are missing. Select a recipe for ghost requirements, or use Automatic. Ghosts never reserve or create stock. Kiln-family and workshop selections are server-validated and saved with the machine. A selected kiln-family recipe restricts production to that output; incompatible ingredients wait. Switching selection resets partial batch progress without consuming ingredients. Automatic kiln-family matching uses recipe-ID order (clay defaults to four bricks); select Terracotta explicitly to fire clay into terracotta. Ghost output follows the same selection as production.
- **Workshop matching:** any active input slot may hold an ingredient. Counts combine across split stacks. Extra quantities of required ingredients stay for later batches; unrelated ingredients block the batch. Automatic mode runs only one unambiguous match; shared materials such as axe/pickaxe requirements require a choice. Selected mode restricts the intended output. Repair/upgrade identity and components come from the actual source equipment, regardless of slot order.
- **Energy feedback:** the fire animates while a kiln-family machine is burning, including residual idle heat. All coal fire windows follow the lit state, including idle burning, and have no hover tooltip so opening a controller does not cover its center. A slim vertical bar beside the fire shows the last consumed coal’s remaining energy, draining downward at the actual idle or productive rate and refilling when more coal is consumed. Hover it for the percentage. Pump/heater productive costs are charged per batch, so their bar steps down on batch completion. The embedded striker to the right of the centered fire lights a cold fire. See [continuous coal fires](#continuous-coal-fires). Liquid gauges retain full names and exact mB tooltips, including expected fluids in empty buffers. Color is supplemental.
- **Material storage:** Kiln, Fertilizer Furnace, Cooking Stove and Foundry have two inputs and two outputs. Tannery/Textile have two interchangeable inputs and two outputs; Smithy has four ingredients and one output. Standard stacks stay 32 each, giving 64 per paired bank. Kiln-family input drains the first slot before refilling it from the second. Finished output stacks remain in place when another output is collected; new batches fill the first compatible slot, splitting across the two slots when needed. Shift-click and hoppers can collect outputs; processing speed and fuel rates do not increase.
- **Persistence:** Kiln-family slots 0/1/2 retain input/fuel/output; backup fuel is 3, backup input is 4 and backup output is 5. A `TwoMaterialSlots` marker distinguishes this expansion from older retired T1 layouts, whose excess contents still return physically above the controller once. Workshop slot 6 remains backup coal; slot 7 is the second output for Tannery/Textile and is unused by the Smithy. Tannery/Textile activate their existing input slot 1. Pump/heater coal slots and T2 output banks are unchanged. Existing stock and in-progress work persist.

`MachineUi` and `MachineScreen` own reusable rendering and recipe browsing; `CivicWidget` uses the same frame and controls. `MachineMenus` shares native inventory placement and shift-click routing; `MachineInventory` shares fuel-bank and whole-output operations. `MachineWork` supplies read-only workshop matching/consumption quotations to both server and UI; the server alone commits fuel and material use. Native furnace processing, finite extraction, fluid transactions and item repair remain specialized rather than being forced into an opaque generic engine. Existing pause/reset and loaded-only production rules still apply.

## JEI recipe discovery

With Just Enough Items installed, recipe lookup (default **R**) and uses (default **U**) include the Kiln, Foundry, Fertilizer Furnace, Cooking Stove, Tannery, Textile Workshop, Smithy, Oil Derrick, Fired Heater, Distillation Column, Air-Cooled Condenser, Coal Drill, canister filling/emptying, saw cutting and recombining. Each category identifies its controller/tool in the tab and catalyst sidebar. Ordinary crafting remains in JEI's Crafting category. Iron-ingot lookup includes a **Foundry** tab; raw iron's uses lead to the same processing recipes. The Foundry also has a short information page explaining the metal-free brick bootstrap.

Pages show ingredient alternatives and counts, coproducts, baseline processing seconds, fuel/tool requirements and relevant operating notes. Fluids use the controller palette with visible amounts and full fluid names/exact mB in tooltips. Coal is shown as a fuel supply, not a false fixed one-Coal ingredient charge: idle burning and thermal efficiency still apply. Drill oil is labeled optional, with its consumption and dry slowdown explained. Repair pages are explicitly half-damaged examples; actual material use and resulting maximum durability depend on the equipment. Existing enchantments and identity are preserved by production.

Kiln-family pages come from the client's synchronized recipe manager, workshop/repair pages from the production catalog, and refinery pages from shared `IndustrialProcess` batches. Cutting/recombining examples run the actual recipe match/assemble methods and preserve material/shape identities in JEI. Retired furnace fuel pages and forbidden equipment anvil/grindstone repairs are hidden; supported enchanted-book application and single-item disenchanting remain. Retired items are removed from the ingredient list.

Click the processing arrow in kiln-family and workshop screens to open that station's JEI category. JEI keeps clear of their recipe drawers. This integration is read-only: it does not auto-fill machine inventories or change the selected server recipe. Select the desired job in the controller. JEI is optional and unnecessary on dedicated servers; the tested API version is pinned in `gradle.properties`.

## Metalworking bridge

The **Foundry** is a coal-fired T1 multiblock that unlocks metal production and construction of T2 machinery. Craft its controller from **8 brick blocks surrounding cobblestone**. Its three-wide brick body has a hollow hearth, cut-brick shoulders and a raised rear chimney. Build with the contextual guide; a stone saw can make every required cut. Neither the kiln nor Foundry construction requires metal. Hoppers are optional automation after metalworking, not a prerequisite.

The Foundry uses the shared cabinet interface: two ingredient slots, two Coal slots and two output slots. It processes one recipe at a time. **Raw iron/copper/gold or their ores → one ingot in 10 seconds**. **One iron ingot → one steel ingot in 40 seconds**. One Coal supplies 20 seconds of furnace heat, so steel takes four times the heat of ore smelting. Heat continues burning while loaded, as in the kiln; a broken structure cancels current heat/work. Ordinary charcoal, wood and vanilla coal are not accepted. Steel now has an original blue-gray cast-ingot sprite. Steel uses coal as both heat and the abstracted carbon source; there is no separate coke or carbon input.

**Two steel ingots + two copper ingots**, arranged diagonally in a 2×2 crafting grid, make **two Machine Parts**. This is the sole assembly intermediate. Its original 32×32 sprite depicts a steel bearing housing, copper collar and short shaft; [art sources and review](../art/assets/machine_parts/asset.json). The Smithy now owns the equipment recipes listed in [town workshops](#town-workshops). Vanilla furnace/blast-furnace metal smelting and equipment-to-nugget recycling are disabled; nonmetal heat recipes such as glass use the Kiln. Ore smelting in the Foundry is intentionally 1:1, without ore multiplication.

Industrial Casing now uses steel instead of iron. Pump, drill, heater, column and condenser controllers require Machine Parts. Existing items are retained. This establishes the production dependency. The workshop package closes the equipment crafting, repair, trading and naturally spawned equipment-drop routes described below. Exploration loot remains available; broader metal-source and special-equipment audits remain later work.

## Town workshops

The coal-era town chain is **Foundry metals + livestock hides/wool → Tannery/Textile Workshop → Smithy equipment and repairs**. Oil is not required for this bootstrap. All three controllers are in the Civilization creative tab and use the existing contextual construction guide. Their open shapes use vanilla/resource-pack bricks, planks, copper and cut pieces: a low tanning vat with a supported drying frame, an upright textile frame/workbench, and a brick hearth/chimney with a side workbench. Tannery and Textile controllers now have original 32× fronts while their construction blocks continue to use the active resource pack.

The **Tannery controller** uses `plank–brick–plank / plank–barrel–plank / cobblestone–cobblestone–cobblestone`. It needs no iron, cauldron or Smithy. Textile and Smithy controllers retain `brick–brick–brick / plank–center–plank / iron–iron–iron`, with copper and cobblestone centers respectively. Controller crafting needs no leather, cloth, shears, anvil or oil; the assembled Smithy requires the anvil listed below.

The **Tannery** stays within a 3×3, four-block-high envelope. Additional build materials: **2 brick blocks, 6 cobblestone/stone-brick wall blocks, 8 plank slabs, 5 plank quarter beams and 2 plank eighth cubes**. A one-block stone plinth with two front brick accents supports a half-height oak rim around an open vat. The two eighth cubes share the rear rim slab cells and connect the rim continuously to the narrow posts above. A three-piece crossbar completes the drying rack; no water, loose hide or copper lining is required. Its original controller face combines brick, dark iron, a small copper fitting and a leather-colored craft strip. Existing shells need the revised plinth, using the guide; contents remain in the controller.

The **Textile Workshop** also stays within a 3×3, four-block-high envelope. Additional build materials: **5 plank slabs, 9 plank quarter beams, 4 plank eighth cubes and 1 copper quarter beam**. Its two-deep upper-slab bench stands on four shared-cell feet. Two narrow rear posts and a three-piece crossbar frame an open center; the copper roller occupies only the supported central cell. The original controller face uses a large ivory woven swatch, muted indigo selvage and restrained iron/copper framing. No cloth block is placed in the structure.

The **Smithy** is a **6×3, 4.5-block-high** broad brick forge with a 2-block-wide, 1.5-block-deep workbench, one block tall, on four eighth-cube feet and a real floor-level anvil alongside it. Additional build materials: **9 full brick blocks, 14 brick slabs, 8 brick quarter beams, 4 brick eighth cubes, 2 plank slabs, 2 plank quarter beams, 4 plank eighth cubes, 1 cobblestone/stone-brick footing and 1 anvil**. The controller occupies front-center of the forge base; the bench and anvil are to its local right (follow the guide), and the anvil footing is one block below ground level. Any anvil wear state and any orientation work. The usable vanilla anvil retains the existing equipment-repair restrictions; repairs still go through the Smithy menu. The clear hearth is two blocks wide and two deep. A recessed vertical-slab back wall, quarter-beam corner piers and slab/eighth-cube coping finish the rear facade. Quarter-beam piers and hood edges, eighth-cube outer shoulders, and paired vertical chimney slabs make the silhouette narrower toward the top. The table uses upper slabs for its front row and upper quarter beams for its half-depth rear row; four lower eighths share those block spaces as feet. The guide recognizes each piece independently and rejects excess cells. No copper worktop or decorative vise is required.

The open hearth uses pack-native coal/fire visuals; fire and chimney smoke run while the fire is lit. This visual coal bed is not inventory, provides no free fuel or extra heat, and never places actual fire. Structural materials use the active resource pack. Existing Smithy, Tannery and Textile shells require manual reshaping with their guides; controllers, contents and saved work are preserved, and no old blocks are automatically removed.

Open the controller and insert materials, or choose a job in the searchable recipe drawer for ghost requirements and a predicted output. Ghosts show missing quantities and cannot be removed as items. The Tannery and Textile Workshop each expose two ingredient slots and two outputs; the Smithy has four interchangeable ingredient slots and one output for one gear piece at a time. Split input stacks combine. Each workshop has two Coal slots. A Smithy with an occupied output pauses the next job until the item is collected or removed by a hopper. Automatic mode starts a unique matching job; ambiguous materials wait for a selection while a lit fire continues its low idle burn. The Repair job locates damaged equipment in any input slot and shows resulting maximum durability. See [shared interfaces](#shared-controller-interfaces) for the interaction contract.

**Equipment coverage:** iron, gold and diamond pickaxes, axes, shovels, hoes, swords and four armor pieces; iron/diamond saws; shears; shields; chainmail armor using iron with the corresponding armor counts; and all nine netherite upgrades. Gold tools have 600 base durability, 7 mining speed and iron-level harvesting; their melee damage sits halfway between matching iron and diamond tools. Gold armor has 264/384/360/312 base durability for helmet/chestplate/leggings/boots, 17.5 protection for a full set and 1 toughness per piece. This puts it above iron and below diamond as a full tier while preserving vanilla gold enchantability. Saws take 3 primary materials, 2 sticks and 1 Leather. The Shield appears under Armor in the recipe drawer, uses the normal iron-rate 1.5 Coal per batch, and can be repaired in the Smithy with iron and Leather. Grade changes its durability, not its blocking behavior. Netherite upgrades consume the diamond base, one netherite ingot, one upgrade template and 1 Leather for tools/weapons or 2 Cloth for armor, taking 30/60 seconds respectively. They preserve identity, enchantments, grade and proportional existing damage. Wooden/stone equipment remains normally craftable. Bows/crossbows, flint and steel, horse armor, tridents, maces and other special equipment are outside the current Smithy catalog; expanding those routes is an explicit follow-up, not a claim that every equipment bypass is closed.

### Equipment grade and repairs

Every damageable item entering a player's inventory without a grade receives one persistent whole-percent roll (normal distribution, mean 75%, standard deviation 20 points); finished Smithy products receive it immediately. Existing and found damageable gear is therefore graded on use. The roll itself has no floor or ceiling. A 100% item has its base stats (the rebalanced tier for gold, vanilla for other materials); the same grade multiplier applies to maximum durability, component-defined mining speed, armor/toughness/knockback resistance including vanilla armor's default attributes, effective melee damage and attack speed, and pre-mitigation projectile damage from graded bows, crossbows and thrown tridents. For example, an iron pickaxe's base mining speed of 6 becomes 3 at 50% grade and 7.5 at 125%; actual block-breaking progress changes with it. Enchantments remain separate. Minecraft requires maximum durability of at least one and positive mining speeds, so mathematically nonpositive grades retain their true percentage in data while those two engine values use the smallest legal representations. Non-numeric special item behaviors and discrete mining capabilities are not grade-scaled; see [open decisions](../open_decisions.md#town-workshop-implementation-details).

The item's tooltip prints its grade. The durability bar stays 13 pixels wide: green shows remaining ordinary durability, black shows durability spent since repair, and gray shows capacity unavailable at the item's grade. Above 100%, one or two pixels at the right end turn cyan for bonus capacity, covering roughly the first 10% of exceptional grade. The cyan segment drains before the ordinary green capacity; spent bonus durability also turns black. Higher grades retain their full actual statistics and durability even though the bar's bonus display is capped. Each item has a saved random wear seed. Up to eight short connected scuff/corrosion layers are selected from 16 programmatically generated variants for that item type as grade falls. Two items of the same type and grade can thus wear differently; repair and upgrade preserve the same pattern while changing how much of it is revealed. Each layer is transparent outside the installed Faithful 32× item silhouette and leaves the base art visible. At 100% or above the item icon is pristine. Existing graded items receive a wear seed when next handled by a player or Smithy. The overlays do not change worn armor on the player model. At 100% grade or above, standard trimmable armor gets one stable, seed-selected vanilla pattern and material; at 99% or lower it has no trim. Repairs that lower grade below 100% remove the trim. Netherite armor upgrades retain the pattern when grade remains exceptional.

**Repair example:** a 100% iron pickaxe with 250 maximum and 220 damage costs 2 iron ingots + 1 Leather. It returns fully repaired at **98% grade and 245 maximum durability**. Primary-material cost is `ceil(0.5 × fresh primary-material count × damage / current maximum)`, minimum one. Grade loss is `max(1, ceil(2 × damage/current maximum × exp((100 − grade)/25)))` points. Weak equipment loses grade faster; there is no separate maximum-durability wear or 25% floor. A Smithy repair is unavailable if the resulting grade would be zero or lower. Enchantments, names and other item identity data remain; repair prior-work cost is cleared and no XP is charged. Netherite uses netherite ingots for repair. The one-unit minimum means some cheap-tool repairs cost as much primary material as replacing the tool; replacement still also needs its fresh bindings/handle ingredients.

**Acquisition and repair boundaries:** vanilla crafting for this catalog, netherite upgrade recipes and all 18 vanilla Smithing Table armor-trim application recipes are disabled. Anvils cannot material-repair or combine catalog equipment; crafting-grid combining and two-item grindstone repairs are disabled for it. Naming, enchanted-book application and single-item disenchanting remain available and do not restore durability. Their ordinary XP rules remain; the no-XP rule is for Smithy repairs. Armor trims now come from exceptional grade, including leather armor, and cannot be applied to ordinary gear in the vanilla table. Existing sub-100% trims are removed when their equipment is next handled. Mending has no effects and no supported items, so existing copies are inert and labeled disabled. Saved and new villager offers for damageable equipment or Mending books are filtered; other trades remain.

Ordinary leather-producing animal loot and piglin leather barter now yield Raw Hide. Its original pelt icon shows mottled brown hide and a pale folded underside, distinct from woven Cloth. Four rabbit hides craft one Raw Hide. Existing Leather and finite treasure leather remain usable. Naturally spawned catalog gear no longer drops from mobs; guaranteed player-supplied/picked-up gear remains recoverable. Other exploration loot is retained. Full husbandry, supplied wool production, rare/special equipment and renewable treasure/material loopholes still need the broader acquisition audit. This package does not add continuous animal hunger or replace the current fertilizer recipe.

## Find a site

Craft a **Prospecting Rod** (two sticks and one iron ingot, diagonal) and right-click ground. Its original icon shows a wooden T-grip and pointed iron probe. It reports the closest large deposit within 192 horizontal blocks, horizontal center, underground Y range and whether the inspected location is within surface extraction reach. It describes the generated location, not an exact remaining-stock count; an emptied site remains discoverable. It does not load chunks or mark the Survey Table. Inspections have a one-second cooldown; immutable site queries share a bounded 4,096-cell cache per level, including empty cells, across generation and prospecting.

- **Coal seams:** windswept uplands, stony/jagged/frozen peaks, snowy slopes and groves, meadows, savanna plateaus, stony shores and badlands variants. There is no separate surface marker; exposed dense coal belongs to the extractable seam itself.
- **Oil fields:** plains/sunflower plains, desert, swamp/mangrove swamp and savanna. One crude-oil source sits directly above the dark seep block at the deposit center. Generation schedules its first fluid update, so it begins spreading without a neighboring block being broken; loading an older one-block seep schedules the same update. It spreads slowly like a placed fluid source, but is not bucket-fillable and does not count as reservoir stock.

For the existing **New Surv** save, a world-root `civilization-retrofit-oil.flag` opts its previously saved chunks into a one-time oil backfill as they load. The backfill uses the same deterministic deposit sites as new terrain, adds the seep and source at an unobstructed natural surface, and replaces only natural underground rock with finite oil or the reservoir cavity. Ores, air tunnels and non-geology construction are left in place, so an edited site may be irregular. Player-placed plain stone cannot be distinguished from natural stone and may be replaced inside a deposit. Each processed oil-site chunk saves a completion marker; later visits and extraction never refill it. Other worlds are unaffected. Unvisited saved chunks are backfilled on their next load, without loading the whole explored world at once. A full copy of New Surv made before enabling the flag is kept under the workspace's ignored `.tools/backups` directory.
- Ordinary coal ore remains hand-mineable and keeps its existing Coal drops. Dense seam and seep blocks cannot be mined in survival, have no item drops and resist explosions/pistons. Surface oil is a visible clue separate from the real resource body underneath; removing it does not restore or deplete the reserve.

Coal uses a one-in-three deterministic roll per 256×256 cell and tries up to four locations. Oil searches the sixteen cells of each 4×4 region in seed-stable shuffled order and places at most one qualifying field there. The roughly two-coal-to-one-oil site frequency is a broad-world target, not a strict local quota; suitable tagged biomes and terrain still determine the actual count. It classifies the biome at the actual surface height and requires modest relief at the center, four cardinal edges and four diagonal edges (at most eight blocks for oil or sixteen for coal). Oil requires dry, solid geology at all sampled points. Coal requires a dry, solid center and at least six of nine sampled body columns to be solid, allowing a riverbank or cave to cut into part of a seam without accepting a mostly open field. A failed location moves to the next candidate; no site is promised in every region. Candidate centers are at least 80 blocks from the nearest cell edge, so even maximum-radius fields remain within their source cell. Each chunk generates its own field slice, and the result is stable for a world seed and generator. Existing generated chunks do not gain the new shape. Coal is an elongated, gently irregular seam **141–161 blocks long and 99–113 wide**. For three fifths of coal candidates, the generator probes natural exposed rock and riverbed inside the footprint and, where a suitable dry-centered seam fits, aligns the real coal layer with that opening. Otherwise it uses a buried layer about 24 blocks below the center surface. Caves can also cut into either layer. This favors natural outcrops without requiring every seam to show; the actual exposed-site fraction remains unmeasured. The seam is 2–3 blocks thick through most of the field and up to five near the middle, with a slight tilt. Oil is a tapered irregular pocket **69–73 blocks long and 55–59 wide**, centered about 32 blocks beneath its seep; it holds one oil block per column at the rim and up to six near the center under a two-block air cap. The long axis may run east–west or north–south. Generation replaces natural rock only, leaving caves and other materials in place, so actual stock may be lower than the ideal geometry. Actual stock is the generated blocks, not a separate reserve count. Permanent-world site frequency and reserve lifetime remain unvalidated. Development-authored small sites still use the old compact geometry.

An Oil Derrick controller may be placed at any height above the underground body's top, provided its horizontal position lies within the deposit's actual footprint. Coal Drills may stand over the broad seam within sixteen blocks of its center surface height. Machines extract from the physical field below that surface location. The initial bore reach is implicit; no separate shaft/pipes are required, and the machines do not quarry ordinary overburden. They remove only matching resource blocks within the generated body. A machine scans from the field center outward and down each local column. It can use a loaded slice even if distant field chunks are unloaded; it never loads them. An incomplete scan does not claim depletion while chunks are missing. All machines encounter the same actual blocks, so removing one cannot pay twice. Breaking/rebuilding a controller or removing exposed coal does not restore resources.

Condenser saddles extend into its rounded underside so the vessel visibly rests on steel supports. Controllers, casings, cooling grilles, flues, ports and pipes share cool-gray steel plates with iron fasteners and restrained wear. Brick hearths use the active resource pack's bricks. The derrick accepts ordinary planks and their exact saw cuts; the default guide shows oak, and a resource pack supplies the chosen wood texture. Retired oil-lift structures need guide-led rebuilding.

### Process and initial tuning

| Machine | Completed batch | Time at 20 TPS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oil Derrick | One real oil height step → 125 mB crude | 2.5 seconds |
| Fired Heater | 250 mB crude → 250 mB heated crude | 5 seconds |
| Distillation Column | 1,000 mB heated crude → 800 mB distillate vapor + 200 mB lubricating oil + 1 sulfur | 20 seconds |
| Air-Cooled Condenser | 200 mB distillate vapor → 200 mB refined fuel | 5 seconds |
| Coal Drill | 80 mB refined fuel → one real dense coal block removed → 16 Coal | 5 seconds lubricated; gradually up to 25 seconds dry |

One Oil Derrick is the nominal throughput unit: **50 mB/s crude**, feeding one heater and one column at 50 mB/s, then one condenser at **40 mB/s refined fuel**. The column also supplies **10 mB/s lubricant and one sulfur per 20 seconds**. Two fully lubricated Coal Drills now use **32 mB/s** together, leaving 8 mB/s for engines when the line is continuously supplied. These are loaded production rates at 20 TPS, with coal available and all outputs clear. Startup fills the batch buffers before steady operation; batches are retained, so downstream flow can pulse. Crude/heated-crude lines reach the 50 mB/s pipe capacity; product lines carry less because the column splits the stream. Empty search stretches, depleted/unloaded deposits, missing fuel or backed-up coproducts can interrupt supply. Keep lubricant and sulfur moving as well as fuel.

One Coal pumps 250 mB; another heats 250 mB. Thus eight coal plus finite crude stock can produce 800 mB engine fuel, 200 mB lubricant and one sulfur. Eight lubricated drill batches use 640 mB of that fuel and 80 mB lubricant to extract 128 coal from a separate finite seam, leaving 160 mB refined fuel for engines or later drilling. This is a geological bootstrap, not renewable energy or a fixed currency exchange rate. Lit pump/heater reserves burn slowly while idle. Charcoal, biomass and raw crude cannot replace the required machine fuels.

The three saleable products have distinct uses: **fuel powers machines, oil lubricates moving machinery, sulfur supports farming**. Sulfur uses an original 32×32 yellow mineral sprite with visible facets and fracture texture; its recipes and stack limits are unchanged. Heated crude and vapor are internal process streams. Coal fires the heater; there is no separate heavy-fuel product or self-fueling refinery loop. Condensation uses air cooling: no water supply, temperature balancing, pressure simulation or electrical network is required in this version.

This is deliberately simplified petroleum processing. Real sulfur recovery and lubricant finishing need additional treatment beyond atmospheric distillation; those steps are folded into the column. Sulfur is a real refinery coproduct and agricultural input, not a substitute for nitrogen. Background: [The Sulphur Institute](https://www.sulphurinstitute.org/about-sulphur/faq/). Ratios are game tuning, not a chemical mass-balance model.

### Lubrication and fertilizer

The **Coal Drill** is the first lubrication consumer. It starts at full lubrication. Each completed dry batch lowers its speed condition by one percentage point, down to a 20% floor. It continues working; it never randomly breaks or destroys parts. Idle time, unloaded chunks and blocked work cause no wear. With at least 10 mB oil available, the next batch runs at full speed, consumes 10 mB, and restores condition to 100%. Adding oil restores performance without a repair recipe. Current other machines do not require lubrication.

**1 Sulfur + 1 Gravel → 1 Sulfur Blend** in crafting, then **1 Sulfur Blend → 8 Mineral Fertilizer** in the Fertilizer Furnace in **20 seconds**, costing one Coal at base efficiency. This is the only current fertilizer production route. Clay is no longer involved. Old Raw Mineral Blend cannot be crafted or fired; its registered item remains for existing saves but is hidden from creative and JEI. Sulfur Blend retains the existing `enriched_mineral_blend` ID and yellow-chip artwork. The fertilizer sack is visual packaging, not an ingredient. This is a game recipe, not a claim that sulfur supplies nitrogen.

## Physical extraction

Coal extraction replaces the targeted dense coal block with air and plays breaking effects. Output enters the machine without duplicate world drops. Underground oil has eight height steps per full block. The derrick drains each block in 125 mB steps from the top of the nearest column downward, then works outward; empty cells become air. Reservoir oil remains stationary and finite: it does not spread, equalize into tunnels or create sources. The separate surface source spreads slowly over terrain and is not an extraction target. Neither reservoir nor surface oil can be collected directly in buckets/canisters. Swimming through crude oil slows horizontal and vertical movement.

Search checks at most 1,024 cells per ten-tick work step. Searching empty stretches adds time. Loaded field slices can be extracted without distant chunks, and an unloaded slice never counts as depleted. Extraction never loads chunks itself.

## Liquids and pipes

Every process buffer holds **4,000 mB**; a Liquid Tank holds **16,000 mB** of one registered industrial fluid. Tanks cannot mix fluids. Crude generates in physical reservoirs with a separate flowing surface clue; no survival item places it. The crude fluid has a slow, eight-frame, 64-pixel animated texture based on the [source and exporter](../art/assets/crude_oil/asset.json). Other process fluids remain contained.

Connect pipes between the flanged ports, tanks and extractor connections listed above. Pipes connect around corners and vertically. Tubes have an eight-sided, half-block-wide profile with a mostly solid steel shell, narrow clear inspection strips and thin flush rings. Compact fittings appear only at bends and branches; the larger selection/collision envelope follows the connected arms. Pipes are always open and have no clickable valve. Existing closed pipe states also conduct. Connections refresh on placement/load, including pipes authored by testing tools. Each pipe has a shared **25 mB per ten-tick step (50 mB/s at 20 TPS)** transfer budget. All successful routes through that segment share the budget, across pushing, pulling, branches and fluid types; a second machine call cannot double it. Transfers are limited by the least remaining capacity along the route and only actual delivered fluid spends capacity. Unused capacity does not accumulate. Separate lines can run independently. Direct adjacent-port operations also move at most 25 mB per call, but have no pipe segment to share a budget. The budget is transient, not saved liquid or pressure. Tanks are passive buffers and do not drive tank-to-tank transfer alone.

Routing explores at most **128 pipes per reachable network**, never forcing chunks. An oversized network transfers nothing through that traversal; break it into shorter runs with buffer tanks. Loops cannot duplicate delivery. Loaded endpoints are visited in deterministic order; fair branch scheduling is not implemented. These are unbuffered conduits: they carry no persistent fluid inventory, and a route can transfer different compatible streams in separate operations. Separate lines are recommended for legibility but are not a hard fluid-lock rule. There are no pumps, pressure loss or filters in the pipe itself. Clear windows show confirmed transfers inside the tube: brown crude, amber refined fuel, orange heated crude, pale vapor and olive lubricating oil. Moving highlights indicate direction; the interior clears completely after 0.8 seconds without transfer. Empty glass has real transparency and sparse edge glints, with no opaque backing. Only the successful route animates, not unused branches. This is brief visual telemetry, not stored liquid or simulated travel time; mixed traffic displays the most recent fluid per pipe arm. Rendering is limited to nearby pipes and does not force chunks.

Remote pump/refinery ports expose only their assigned controller buffer, only on the outward face, while that controller and its whole structure remain present. Cached capabilities recheck validity and claims. A port cannot simultaneously bind to two complete vessels.

An empty/full canister occupies one slot and transfers **exactly 1,000 mB** by right-clicking the controller or tank. Crude, refined fuel and lubricating oil have canisters. Heated crude/vapor cannot become canisters or bypass refining. Empty canisters withdraw a portable output first, then lubricant, then input. The drill accepts lubricant into its separate oil buffer. Wrong liquid or insufficient room/amount changes nothing. Canisters currently have no automated fluid-item capability.

Every automatic transfer edge checks claim ownership: both ends must be unclaimed or have the same owner. Remote ports also check the controller-to-port boundary. Manual use and inventories honor owner/whitelist access. Resource extraction separately checks the actual coal/oil block: unclaimed resources are accessible, claimed resources require a controller claim with the same owner. Owning a controller does not reserve the entire deposit.

## Stops, saves and limits

A missing structure, wrong site, physically depleted deposit, unloaded deposit chunks, protected resource, missing input/fuel or full output pauses processing. Blocked batches consume no deposit stock or productive fuel cost; partial progress resets when blocked. Lit coal machines still spend their low idle burn. Productive coal heat credit is charged on completion. A coal block always yields 16 coal; an oil height step always yields 125 mB. Neither machine can manufacture output after the corresponding physical resource has gone.

Inventory, all fluid buffers, lubrication, remaining heat, in-progress work and search cursor persist in the block entity. Depletion persists as removed coal blocks and changed oil fluid heights in ordinary chunk saves. The former deposit SavedData counter is no longer used. Rebuilding starts a bounded search through what physically remains. Breaking a controller drops its ordinary item inventory but discards fluids and heat; **drain it first**. A tank item never carries its stored liquid, so it cannot become a portable bulk container. There is no offline production or forced chunk loading.

Generator/biome rules should stay fixed within a world; development can create new worlds when they change. Crash-atomic saving across player inventory, machine chunks and resource chunks is not implemented. As with other current production systems, journals are diagnostic rather than an authoritative transaction database.

The refinery master and its reference-driven edits live in `art/textures/sources`; `art/textures/block_families.json` owns protected edit rectangles and the shared palette. Prepare/check/publish the `refinery` family, then regenerate geometry with `python tools/generate_refinery_models.py`. See the [art workflow](../art/textures/README.md). Liquid canisters use native workshop-tin models with original engine-family iron/steel/brass and enamel labels: gray ring for empty, charcoal drop for crude, amber flame for refined fuel, green oil-can for lubricant. All four share geometry and a material atlas; capacity, recipes and transfers are unchanged. Deposit distribution, throughput and freight economics need actual play/load evidence before a permanent public world.

Verification uses the normal server suite and the hidden `deposits` visual scene: generated body shape across chunk boundaries, real machine output conservation, and before/after coal and oil views in a disposable quarter cutaway. The oil check drains a bounded local sample rather than the entire field. The `industry` scene checks a pipe-connected heater/column/condenser delivering fuel, synchronized menus, contextual guides and Photon/Faithful appearance. Current test/deployment facts: [project status](../docs/status.md).

Industrial Guardrails remain available as decorative blocks but are not required by the column. Existing decks, railings and ladders may be removed manually; the update does not delete placed decoration. Existing condensers need the two lower center saddle pieces replaced with full Industrial Casing blocks, as shown by the updated guide.

Authored testing maps may store explicit finite-site geometry without changing natural generation or introducing stock counters; the [campus reference](testing.md#user-testing-campus) documents the bounded format and save requirements.

## Hot-Bulb Engine

A standalone hot-bulb engine with a shared original iron/steel/brass atlas, reused steel casing, a continuous stepped cast-iron bed, faceted horizontal cylinder, enclosed beveled crankcase and a fully rotating four-spoke flywheel. The working assembly sits one model unit lower, with a continuous cast bed replacing the two stands. The cylinder is 24 model units long (one third longer than the earlier version); the crankcase and wheel sit six units farther forward. The native modeling revision adds a thinner steel-faced rim, layered access covers/head fasteners, recessed exhaust and a raised readable dial with a physical lever; these are visual details, not new controls or construction requirements. Current consumer is its timed commissioning test; external boat/dock consumer integration follows in the freight package. Existing boats still use their helm-integrated drive. This is not an electric grid or shaft simulation.

**Construction:** make the controller at the Smithy from **4 Steel Ingots, 2 Machine Parts, 2 Copper Ingots and 4 Iron Ingots**, taking **60 seconds** of productive smithy work. Craft an Engine Cylinder with `III / P P / III` (I iron ingot, P Machine Parts); craft an Engine Flywheel with four Steel Ingots in a cross surrounding one Machine Part. The contextual guide requires **8 Industrial Casing slabs, 1 Engine Cylinder and 1 Engine Flywheel**. The controller occupies the front-center of a complete 3×3 half-height bed; cylinder and flywheel automatically match its facing when placed in their required positions, regardless of which side you stand on. Away from an engine build, they face the player normally. Keep the marked space above and in front of the machinery and behind the cylinder clear. The controller and cylinder models join into one continuous cast-iron bed, with the crankcase extended over the control face; a short shaft supports the overhung flywheel from the crankcase, leaving its rim clear. Saws supply the existing half-block forms. **Existing engines:** fill both front corners with casing slabs, reroute fuel to the front and lubricant underneath, and clear the extended machinery guide. The old rear copper slab is still unnecessary. No blocks or stored liquids are removed automatically.

**Supply:** controller front accepts refined fuel and its underside accepts lubricating oil (route the lubricant pipe beneath the bed). Existing pipes and sided fluid capabilities support filling/draining; capacities are **4,000 mB fuel / 2,000 mB oil**, including reserved fractional credit. Filled canisters insert 1,000 mB. Empty canisters withdraw fuel; sneak-use withdraws oil. Canisters require a whole 1,000 mB; use pipes to remove smaller quantities. Crude and wrong liquids are rejected. Filling never silently consumes a cargo tank. Place a controller to see the material guide; open it for the shared cabinet interface, named fluid gauges, Start/Stop and Test Run.

**Operation:** Start spends **10 mB fuel once**, then heats for **5 loaded seconds**. With less than 10 mB it waits without spending fuel. Ready/neutral has no idle fuel drain. Test Run applies full load for **10 seconds**, dissipating that work rather than producing an item; it spends **20 mB fuel and 1 mB lubricant** when fully supplied. At full productive load the common work model uses 2 mB fuel/s and 0.1 mB oil/s. Dry operation gradually reduces delivered power to 20%; supplied lubrication restores efficiency. Stop cancels startup/readiness and the test; a subsequent cold start costs fuel again. The startup flame goes out when ready; loaded operation turns the complete flywheel and produces bounded exhaust and quiet native mechanical sounds.

Loose flywheels and construction guides use the matching static model. A placed controller selects the animated version; removing it restores the static wheel within one loaded second. Item icons use fitted inventory transforms. These are visual states, not additional construction requirements.

**State and limits:** tanks, fractional work credit, lubrication, enabled state and completed warmup persist; a test run does not restart after load. No offline work or forced chunks. A missing structure stops work and requires reheating. The reusable server work request allows at most one accepted request per game tick and checks local range, loaded endpoints, structure and claim boundaries. The test harness is the only production caller for now; future boat/dock adapters must establish consumer ownership and cannot treat this as an unrestricted power aura. Physics substeps must not independently spend fuel or create extra output.

Normal controller break returns an empty controller; fluid contents and sub-mB work credit are **lost**, not packed into the item. Drain service tanks first. There is no explosion or fluid-spill simulation. Existing claim checks govern menus, canisters and pipe boundaries. More elaborate salvage/dismantling is deferred; no boat build allowance or cargo tank behavior changed in this step.

## Production coal balance

The standard production coal budget is four times the original baseline. `ProductionEnergy` owns one 400-unit coal budget: kilns and standard workshop jobs use one unit per heat/work tick; the Tannery and Textile Workshop use one unit per two work ticks. Equipment's longer durations create their material-scaled total cost. Pumping and heating cost eight units per five mB (250 mB per coal). Other non-equipment recipe times and all output quantities stay the same; equipment durations scale by material as described above. One coal makes two ingots, eight brick items, two cooked meals, eight fertilizer, four Leather from Raw Hide, or eight Cloth from four Wool; steel needs two coal per ingot. Pumping and heating 1,000 mB together cost eight coal. Claims, crafting costs and refined-fuel consumption are unchanged.

The listed yields assume uninterrupted productive work. Lit idle time also spends coal under the shared fire rules below. Remaining heat credits persist on save/load.

Production rates above are the neutral-temperature baseline. [Environmental heat](heat.md) modifies newly purchased kiln/workshop work credit and heater batch cost; recipe duration and yields remain unchanged. Fuelled controllers also emit finite waste heat.

## Continuous coal fires

The Brick Kiln, Fertilizer Furnace, Cooking Stove, Foundry, Tannery, Textile Workshop, Smithy, Oil Derrick and Fired Heater share a manual fire lifecycle. Add Coal, complete the structure and click the flint-and-steel button inside the cabinet, to the right of the fire. Each accepted strike costs **1 kcal** of labor at neutral comfort and has a **one-in-three chance** to light; the server permits at most **one strike per second**. Unlucky accepted strikes cost labor but no coal. Rejected clicks (cooldown, lit, wet, incomplete or unfueled) cost nothing. The cost is configurable as `machineIgnitionKcal` and follows the ordinary comfort modifier. The integrated striker needs no separate item. A brief contact/sparks frame and native flint sound acknowledge the strike; successful ignition adds a fire sound. The button disables while lit.

Once lit, the fire automatically feeds from both coal slots, even without materials or with blocked output. Productive costs remain as listed above. Idle burn is **one coal per 200 loaded seconds** at neutral temperature: 10% of the kiln/workshop active rate. Pump/heater use the same per-coal idle lifetime while retaining their existing productive batch costs. Kiln/workshop thermal efficiency modifies purchased reserves, including their idle lifetime. Each coal adds a finite work reserve; its environmental heat is released as that reserve is spent. Idle fires therefore warm gently, while active jobs heat faster according to coal demand. Pump/heater productive heat is charged at batch completion. Idle burn generates no products or free energy.

Exhausting both the burning reserve and available coal extinguishes the fire. Adding coal afterward requires another successful strike. Breaking the structure extinguishes and discards its burning reserve; unloading pauses it without offline catch-up. Lit state, reserve, fractional idle drain and strike cooldown persist. Existing saves without fire state start unlit, retaining any saved heat until manually struck. There is no separate stop button: remove spare coal and let the reserve burn out, or dismantle the structure. Fuel purchases use the shared `coal_fire_fuel` diagnostic event.

Cloth uses an original 32×32 folded-wool item sprite with visible woven variation and an indigo selvage, replacing the white-wool slab placeholder. Its blue edging is visual identity, not a dye input or a new cloth variant. [Asset record](../art/assets/cloth/asset.json).

## Production experience

Kiln-family machines (Kiln, Cooking Stove, Foundry and Fertilizer Furnace) accrue recipe XP and use native furnace collection: taking either output awards accumulated XP; hopper extraction leaves it banked, and breaking the controller releases it. Recipe history persists across saves. Vanilla-equivalent outputs use vanilla smelting rates (cooked fish/meat 0.35, iron/copper 0.7, gold 1 XP per batch); four-brick batches award 1.2 XP. Custom baking, steel and fertilizer recipes award 0.35 XP per batch. Fractional awards use vanilla probabilistic rounding.

Tannery, Textile Workshop and Smithy accrue 1 XP per completed production batch, including equipment upgrades, but not repairs. Both Tannery/Textile outputs and the Smithy's single output support normal and shift-click collection. Their saved XP bank survives hopper extraction and is released on player collection or controller removal. Idle burning, incomplete/blocked jobs, item transfers, resource extraction and fluid processing award no XP. Previously collected zero-XP production is not reconstructed; kiln-family recipe history that remains unclaimed uses the updated recipe rates.
